# How To Use Build That Wall

## 1. Welcome to Build That Wall

Build That Wall is the free online version of the outrageous political strategy card game: open the website, press Play, and you are in a game in under a minute. This guide walks through every screen, button, setting and card, so a brand-new player can learn everything without anyone to ask.

The game is played at [almapeterson.github.io/build-that-wall](https://almapeterson.github.io/build-that-wall/). It is the same game as the real card game sold on Amazon, with the same 107-card deck, the same rules and the same artwork.

### 1.1 What the game is about

Two to six players race to build the most miles of wall. Each turn you draw 3 cards and take up to 3 actions. An action is building a wall section or playing a card. Matching wall sections are worth more together, so the heart of the game is collecting matching sets.

Your rivals are not sitting still. They take your built sections with Debt, send you into Quarantine so you skip a turn, steal cards from your hand and hand out their own unwanted cards to you. You defend yourself with Self-Defense and Savings cards. When the game ends, the player with the most miles of wall wins.

### 1.2 What you need

You need only a device with a modern web browser and an internet connection.

| Device | Works? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Computer (Windows, Mac, Chromebook, Linux) | Yes | Any recent Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. Mouse or touchpad. |
| Tablet (iPad, Android) | Yes | Best held sideways (landscape). Tap to play. |
| Phone (iPhone, Android) | Yes | Turn the phone sideways. The game asks you to if you hold it upright. |

There is nothing to download or install, and no account is needed to play. Everything you need is on the website.

### 1.3 What it costs

Playing is free: every game mode, every card, the tutorial and the instructions cost nothing. An optional Gold Membership adds extras such as holiday card backs and leaderboards. Payments are turned off right now (the store says "Reason: Not Ready For Payments Yet"), but you can still try Gold free for 7 days once you sign in.

### 1.4 How to use this guide

The guide follows the order you meet things on the website. Read it front to back to learn everything, or jump to the section you need:

- **Sections 2 to 3:** the home page and setting your name.
- **Sections 4 to 8:** starting a game, the three ways to play, and the six ways a game can end.
- **Sections 9 to 14:** playing a game: the screen, your turn, building walls, every card, blocking, and the messages you see.

- **Sections 16 to 18:** Settings, Account & Store, and the built-in Tutorial and Instructions.

Button names appear in **bold**, exactly as they are written on the screen. Words in quotation marks are messages the game shows you.

### 1.5 A first game in five steps

If you just want to start playing right now, this is all you need:

1. Open [almapeterson.github.io/build-that-wall](https://almapeterson.github.io/build-that-wall/).
2. Press **Play**.
3. Press **Play with bots**.
4. Press **Start**.
5. When it says it is your turn, press **OK**, then tap a card in your hand to see what you can do with it. Press **End Turn** when you are finished.

Turn on **Tutorial** (the button on the right of the game screen) and a yellow tip box suggests a good move every turn and explains why.

## 3. Setting your name

Your name is how other players see you: on the scoreboard, in online rooms and on the leaderboard. Setting it takes three taps, and you can change it any time.

### 3.1 Set your name on the home page

1. Tap the tan box at the top of the home page that says **✎ Set your name** (or, if you already have a name, the line that says **Playing as … ✎**).
2. A small form appears in its place: the words **Your name**, a text box, and two buttons, **Save** and **Cancel**. The text box is ready for typing straight away, and on a phone the keyboard opens by itself.
3. Type your name.
4. Press **Save** (or press Enter on a keyboard, or **Done** on a phone keyboard).

![The name form](guide/02-name-form.png)

*Step 2: the name form, ready for typing.*

![The name form with a name typed](guide/03-name-typed.png)

*Step 3: a name typed in, ready to Save.*

After you press **Save**, the form closes and the top of the page says **Playing as** followed by your name in red, with a small pencil (✎) after it.

If you press **Cancel** instead, the form closes and nothing changes. If you had no name before, the tan **Set your name** box comes back and you are still called "Player".

### 3.2 What a name can be

- **Up to 20 characters.** The text box stops you typing more.
- **Letters, numbers, spaces and symbols are all fine,** including emoji and letters from any language.
- **Spaces at the start or end are removed.** Typing "  Grandma  " saves "Grandma".
- **A name can't be empty.** Pressing **Save** with nothing typed shows "Type a name first."

### 3.3 Names when you are signed in

If you are signed in (section 17.1), your name is saved to your account, so it follows you to every device. Two signed-in players can't have the same name, which keeps the leaderboards fair.

| Message | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Name saved." | It worked. | Nothing. |
| "Someone already has that name. Try another." | Another signed-in player already uses that name. | Pick a different name, for example add a number or your town. |
| "Your sign-in has expired. Sign in again to save your name." | Your sign-in ran out. The game opens the sign-in box for you. | Sign in again, and the name saves. |
| "Saved here, but the game server didn't answer." | The name is saved on this device, but not yet on your account. | Nothing now. Try saving it again later so it reaches your other devices. |

If you are not signed in, your name is saved on this device only. You never see "taken" because the check only applies to signed-in players.

### 3.4 The other place to change your name

The **Account & Store** page also has a **Your name** box with a **Save name** button (section 17.1). It does exactly the same thing as the home page form: whichever you use, the other shows the new name.

### 3.5 Where your name shows

- **Against bots:** you are the first player, shown with your name. The bots are named "BOT" followed by a name such as Freedom, Maverick or Liberty.
- **Pass'n'Play:** your name is filled in as the first player's name. You type the other players' names before the game starts (section 6.3).
- **Online:** other players see your name in the waiting room and during the game. The Play online page reminds you: "You'll play as *your name*. Change it on the home page."
- **Leaderboards:** your scores are recorded under your name.

A name changed in the middle of a game takes effect from your next game.

Every step after the first has a **Back** button at the bottom left. It takes you one step back, to the **How do you want to play?** box, without losing what you have chosen.

## 5. Play with bots

Play with bots puts you against 1 to 5 computer players on your own device: the quickest way to play, and the best way to learn. You choose how many bots, how hard they are, and how the game ends, then press **Start**.

### 5.1 Step by step

1. On the home page, press **Play**.
2. Press **Play with bots**. The **Play with bots** box opens.
3. Set **How many bots?** with the **−** and **+** buttons. The number between them is how many bots you play against, from 1 to 5.
4. Under **How hard are the bots?**, press **Easy**, **Medium**, **Hard** or **Random**. The one you pick turns red.
5. Optionally, press **Set each bot separately** to give each bot its own level (section 5.4).
6. Optionally, press **More options** to choose how the game ends (section 5.5).
7. Press **Start**.

![The Play with bots box](guide/06-play-bots.png)

*The Play with bots box: 2 bots, both Hard.*

The game screen opens, the cards are shuffled and dealt, and the game begins. A random player goes first, so sometimes a bot starts and you watch its turn before yours.

### 5.2 How many bots?

The number of bots sets the size of the game: 1 bot makes a 2-player game, 5 bots make a 6-player game (the most the game allows).

| Bots | Players | What it is like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | Fast, one-on-one. Every attack is aimed at you, and every one of yours hits your only rival. |
| 2 | 3 | Quick, with a little more going on. A good first game. |
| 3 | 4 | The game's sweet spot: 3 or 4 players is best. |
| 4 | 5 | Busy. Longer waits between your turns. |
| 5 | 6 | The full table: the longest games and the most chaos. |

The **−** button is greyed out at 1 bot and the **+** button at 5 bots, because you can't go below or above those.

### 5.3 How hard are the bots?

The buttons set every bot to the same level. **Insane** is greyed out without Gold; if a game is started with Insane bots after Gold has ended, they play as Hard.

| Level | How the bot plays | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Easy** | Only builds walls, and makes careless choices: half the time it doesn't pick the best wall, and sometimes it skips building altogether. It never plays Debt or other action cards. It blocks your attacks only about half the time. | Your first few games, young players, a relaxed game. |
| **Medium** | Builds sensibly, plays Debt on your walls, and plays its other cards when it can. Now and then it makes a less-than-best choice. | Most players, most of the time. |
| **Hard** | Plays to win. It watches who is leading and aims its Debt at the leader. It finishes pairs into sets first, protects its pairs with Trump Train when you have been attacking, and plays its strongest cards in the best order. | A real challenge, once you know the cards. |
| **Insane ★** | Gold members only. Before every move it tries each move it could make in quick practice games and picks the one that works out best. It plays fair: in practice it shuffles the cards it can't see, so it never knows your hand. It thinks for a moment before each move. | Strong players who find Hard too easy. |
| **Random** | Each bot gets Easy, Medium or Hard at random when the game starts, so you don't know which is which. (Random never picks Insane.) | Variety and surprise. |

All bots, whatever their level, must play Play Immediately cards when they draw them, and all of them can block your attacks with Self-Defense and Savings.

#### The bots learn how you play

Bots remember how each player plays on your device, by name. Over several games they learn things like how often you play Debt and how many miles you usually build. Hard bots use this: if you often play Debt, they protect their pairs sooner. This memory stays on your device; it is not shared with anyone.

### 5.4 Set each bot separately

With 2 or more bots, a blue link **Set each bot separately** appears under the level buttons.

1. Press **Set each bot separately**. The four buttons are replaced by one row per bot: **Bot 1**, **Bot 2**, and so on, each with a drop-down menu.
2. Open each drop-down and choose **Easy**, **Medium**, **Hard** or **Random** for that bot.
3. To go back to one level for all bots, press **Same for every bot**. Every bot then takes Bot 1's level.

![Each bot set separately](guide/07-play-bots-each.png)

*After Set each bot separately: one drop-down per bot.*

Mixing levels makes a good teaching game: for example one Hard bot to show strong play and one Easy bot so a new player isn't always last.

#### Naming the bots

Press **Name the bots** (under the bot levels) to give each bot a name of your own, up to 16 characters. The game shows it as "Bot" and your name, for example **Bot Rex**. Leave a box empty for the bot's usual name (Bot Freedom, Bot Maverick, and so on). Rude words are swapped for polite ones. Your bot names are remembered for next time, and they are used in Pass'n'Play, in Just watch bots play and for the bots in online rooms you host.

### 5.5 More options: how the game ends

Under the bot settings is a blue link **More options**, followed in grey by how the game ends now, for example "(game ends: 3 Sets of 3 Walls)".

1. Press **More options**. A row **The game ends with** appears with a drop-down menu.
2. Choose one of the six endings: **3 Sets of 3 Walls**, **Exhaust Deck**, **Whatever Happens First**, **Whatever Happens Last**, **First to 50 Miles** or **Timed Game**.
3. For Exhaust Deck, Whatever Happens First and Whatever Happens Last, a second row **The deck runs out** appears: choose **Once** or **Twice**. For Timed Game, a row **How long?** appears: choose 10 to 60 minutes.

![More options open](guide/08-play-bots-more.png)

*More options open: The game ends with.*

Section 8 explains each ending in detail and which to pick. Most players leave it on 3 Sets of 3 Walls.

### 5.6 During a bot game

- **Bots play by themselves.** When it is a bot's turn, you watch its cards fly across the board. Short messages at the top of the board say what it did, for example "Bot Liberty discarded a Man Hour."
- **No catching up needed.** Because you watch everything happen, there is no "While you were away" box when your turn starts.
- **Bots may ask you things.** If a bot attacks you and you hold a card that can block it, a question box asks whether you want to use it (section 13).
- **You can speed bots up or slow them down** with the Card animations setting (section 16.3).
- **Watch videos while you wait.** During the bots' turns, a **Watch videos** button lets you browse the cards (section 15).

### 5.7 The Tutorial game

The **Tutorial** button on the home page opens the Tutorial: the rules, then a picture tour of the website. Its last page has a **Start a tutorial game** button, which starts a game against bots with two differences: you always go first, and Tutorial tips are switched on, so a yellow box suggests a good move every turn and explains why (section 18.3).

## 6. Pass'n'Play

Pass'n'Play lets 2 to 6 people share one phone, tablet or computer, passing it on after each turn, with bots in the empty seats if you like. A full-screen pass screen between turns makes sure nobody sees anyone else's cards.

### 6.1 Step by step

1. On the home page, press **Play**.
2. Press **Pass'n'Play**. The **Pass'n'Play** box opens.
3. Set **How many people?** with **−** and **+**: how many real people will share this device (2 to 6).
4. Set **Bots too?** with **−** and **+**: how many bots join (0 up to the seats left; people plus bots can't go over 6).
5. If you added bots, set how hard they are (section 5.3), or each bot separately (section 5.4).
6. Optionally, choose how the game ends under **More options** (section 8).
7. Press **Next**. The **Who's playing?** box opens.
8. Type each person's name (section 6.3).
9. Press **Start**.

![The Pass'n'Play box](guide/10-passnplay.png)

*The Pass'n'Play box: 3 people and 1 Hard bot.*

### 6.2 People and bots together

| People | Bots allowed | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 0 to 4 | Two of you against one Hard bot makes a lively 3-player game. |
| 3 | 0 to 3 | Three of you, no bots: the classic family game. |
| 4 | 0 to 2 | Four of you plus one Easy bot. |
| 5 | 0 to 1 | Five of you, or five plus one bot. |
| 6 | 0 | A full table of people. |

When you raise **How many people?**, the bots go down if needed so the total stays at 6 or under.

### 6.3 Who's playing?

The **Who's playing?** box says "Type each player's name. The game will use them when it's someone's turn." It has one text box per person, labelled **Player 1**, **Player 2** and so on.

- **Player 1 is you.** If you set your name on the home page, it is already filled in. Changing it here changes your home page name too.
- **Empty boxes are fine.** A box left empty shows its placeholder name, so that person plays as "Player 2", "Player 3" and so on.
- **Up to 20 characters each.**
- **On a phone,** the keyboard's **Next** key moves to the next box and the last box's **Go** key starts the game.
- **The names are remembered.** Next time, the same names are filled in for you.

![The Who's playing box](guide/11-whos-playing.png)

*The Who's playing? box. Player 1 is filled in with your name.*

Press **Start** to begin, or **Back** to return to the Pass'n'Play box.

### 6.4 The pass screen

Whenever the game needs a different person to take the device, a full-screen blue pass screen covers everything. It shows:

- **PASS THE DEVICE** at the top, with a picture of a device being handed over.
- **Pass the device to** and the next person's name in big letters.
- **Why**, in one line: what last happened to them and "It's your turn.", or "You have a decision to make." if they need to answer a question during someone else's turn.
- **EVERYONE ELSE: NO PEEKING!**
- A big button: **I'm *Name* - show me**.

![The pass screen](guide/70-pass-screen.png)

*The pass screen. Only the person named should press the button.*

The device vibrates briefly when the pass screen appears (on phones that support it). Hand the device to the person named. When they are holding it, they press **I'm *Name* - show me** (or press Enter or Space on a keyboard). Only then do their cards appear.

The button ignores taps in the first moment after the screen appears, so a tap meant for the last screen can't skip it by accident.

### 6.5 When the pass screen appears

- **At the start of each person's turn.** When one person ends their turn and the next player is a person, the pass screen asks for that person.
- **When someone has a decision to make during another player's turn.** For example, if Sam plays Debt on Alex's wall and Alex holds a Savings card, the pass screen asks for Alex, Alex answers the question, and then the pass screen asks for Sam again so Sam can carry on.
- **Not for bots.** A bot's turn needs nobody to take the device, so bots just play. The device stays with whoever had it until a person is next.

### 6.6 While you were away

After **I'm *Name* - show me**, a box titled **WHILE YOU WERE AWAY** lists what happened to that person since their last turn: cards taken from them, walls lost or gained, attacks blocked, and so on. Each line has a picture of the card that caused it, and walls lost or gained show as small cards marked **Lost** or **Got**.

Read it, then press **OK, it's my turn**. Your cards are drawn and your turn starts.

![While you were away](guide/71-while-you-were-away.png)

*The While you were away box: here, a bot played Democrat Border, so this player has only 1 action.*

If nothing happened to you, the box doesn't appear.

### 6.7 Tips for a smooth Pass'n'Play game

- **Hold the device so others can't see the screen** while it's your turn.
- **Use a tablet if you have one.** Cards are bigger and easier to read.
- **Hand the device over before the next person presses the button.** The next person should press **I'm *Name* - show me** themselves.
- **Turn on Tutorial tips** for new players: the tip box only shows for whoever is holding the device.
- **Watch videos while you wait** is shown to whoever is holding the device during bots' turns.

### 6.8 Pass'n'Play inside an online game

You can also mix the two: two people share one device in an online game while friends play on theirs. That is set up on the online screen, not here (section 7.6).

## 7. Play online

Play online lets everyone play on their own device, wherever they are: join strangers with Quick match, or open a private room and share its 5-letter code with friends. One player is the host: they open the room, choose the bots and the ending, and press **Start game**.

### 7.1 The Play online page

Press **Play**, then **Play online**. The **Play Online** page opens, with a blue **Back** button at the top left (back to the home page) and two cards.

| Card | Button | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quick match:** "Join a game that's waiting for players, or open one for others to join." | **Find a game** | Joins the fullest open game that is waiting for players. If there is none, opens a new public game for others to find. |
| **Play with friends:** "Open a private room and tell your friends its code." | **Open a room** | Opens a private room and shows its code. You are the host. |
| (same card) | **Room code** box + **Join** | Joins a friend's room with the code they gave you. |

![The Play Online page](guide/15-online-page.png)

*The Play Online page.*

The Quick match card also reminds you which name you will play under: "You'll play as *your name*. Change it on the home page."

### 7.2 Quick match: play with whoever is there

1. Press **Find a game**. The page says "Connecting to the online service...", then "Looking for an online game...".
2. If a game is waiting for players, you join it and see the **Waiting room** (section 7.5).
3. If no game is waiting, the website opens a new public game, with you as the host. You see **Your room** (section 7.4) and wait for others to find it.

Quick match games have no code to share: other players find them with their own **Find a game** button.

### 7.3 Play with friends: host a private room

1. Press **Open a room**. The page says "Opening a room...", then shows **Your room**.
2. Under **Room code:** is a 5-letter code in big blue letters, for example **QVPMQ**.
3. Tell your friends the code: say it out loud, text it, or send it any way you like. Codes never use the letters I, L or O or the numbers 0 or 1, so they are easy to read out.
4. Wait for your friends to join. Each one appears in the **Players** list as they arrive.
5. Choose bots and the ending (section 7.4), then press **Start game**.

#### Your own room (Gold)

Gold members also see **Your own room ★** on the Play online page. Its code starts with G (for example **GKX7RD**) and stays the same every time you press **Open my room**, so your friends can keep it and join every week without being told a new code. Press **New code** to get a different one. The code is saved with your account, so it is the same on all your devices. If the room is already open on another device, it says "That room is already open on another device."

### 7.4 Your room (the host's view)

As the host you see **Your room** with these parts, top to bottom:

- **Room code:** the code to share (private rooms only).
- **Players (n):** everyone in the room, with "host (you)" beside your name. Gold members have a gold ★ before their name. With bots, the count shows them too, for example "Players (2 + 1 bot)".
- **More people on this device?:** add people who will share your device (section 7.6).
- **Bots:** with **-** and **+** buttons. Each bot you add gets its own row, **Bot 1:**, **Bot 2:** and so on, with a drop-down to choose **Easy**, **Medium**, **Hard**, **Insane ★** (only when the host has Gold) or **Random**. Bots get the names you gave them under Name the bots (section 5.4). Bots fill seats up to 6 players in all.
- **The game ends with:** a drop-down with the six endings, and **The deck runs out: Once / Twice** for the endings that use the deck (section 8). Everyone in the room sees what you pick. It starts from the ending you last chose under More options, and your choice is remembered.
- **Start game:** starts the game for everyone. It is greyed out until there are at least 2 players (people or bots).
- **Close room:** closes the room and sends everyone back.

## 8. How the game ends

You choose one of six endings before the game starts. Whichever you pick, once the end is reached everyone else gets one final turn, and then the player with the most miles of wall wins.

### 8.1 Where to choose the ending

- **Play with bots, Pass'n'Play, Just watch bots play:** press the blue **More options** link in the setup box, then choose from **The game ends with**.
- **Play online:** the host chooses from **The game ends with** in **Your room**. Everyone else sees the choice in the Waiting room.

The choice is remembered for next time, in both places.

### 8.2 The six endings at a glance

| Ending | The end is reached when… | Game length | Draw pile running out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **3 Sets of 3 Walls** (default) | a player completes 3 wall sets | Medium to long | Never ends the game: the discard pile is shuffled into a new draw pile |
| **Exhaust Deck** | the draw pile runs out (once or twice) | Fixed, medium | Ends the game |
| **Whatever Happens First** | 3 sets OR the deck runs out, whichever is first | Shortest | Can end the game |
| **Whatever Happens Last** | 3 sets AND the deck has run out, both | Longest | Ends it only once 3 sets are also done |
| **First to 50 Miles** | a player has 50 miles of wall or more | Medium | Never ends the game |
| **Timed Game** | the chosen time (10 to 60 minutes) has been played | You choose | Never ends the game |

### 8.3 3 Sets of 3 Walls

This is the standard way to play. The end is reached when any player completes their third full wall set: three matching sections, three times over (for example three Wall Section 2s, three Wall Section 9s and three Wall Section 14s).

The draw pile never ends this game. Whenever it runs out, the discard pile is shuffled to make a new draw pile, and play carries on.

**Why play it:** it rewards building matching sets, which are worth the most (16 miles each) and can never be taken. Games can run long when players keep taking each other's pairs with Debt.

**Watch out for:** the player closest to 3 sets. Use Debt on their pairs before they become sets.

### 8.4 Exhaust Deck

The end is reached when the draw pile runs out. A big **LAST CARD!** notice says "The last card was drawn! Everyone else gets one final turn." Sets don't matter for ending this game.

**The deck runs out: Once or Twice.** With **Once**, the first time the draw pile is empty is the end. With **Twice**, the first time it runs out the game says "The draw pile ran out (1 of 2 times). The discard pile is shuffled in." and play carries on; the second time is the end.

**Why play it:** the game has a set length, so it suits a shorter game or a game with a time limit. Players can see the end coming by watching the draw pile's count.

**Watch out for:** the draw pile number. Build what you can before it reaches zero. Cards that draw extra cards (Trump, Trump Train, Supreme Court Nominee) make the deck run out faster.

### 8.5 Whatever Happens First

The end is reached at whichever comes first: a player completes 3 sets, or the draw pile runs out (once or twice, as chosen).

**Why play it:** the shortest and most unpredictable game. A player racing for sets can end it suddenly, and so can the deck.

**Watch out for:** both at once: the leader's walls and the draw pile.

### 8.6 Whatever Happens Last

The end is reached only once both have happened: a player has completed 3 sets, and the draw pile has run out (once or twice).

- **If the deck runs out first,** the game says so, everyone gets their final turns, and then play carries on until someone completes 3 sets.
- **If someone completes 3 sets first,** play carries on until the draw pile runs out.

**Why play it:** the longest game, and the most forgiving. Players who fall behind have the most time to catch up.

### 8.7 First to 50 Miles

The end is reached when any player has **50 miles** of wall or more at the end of a move. A **FINAL ROUND!** notice says who reached 50 miles, and everyone else gets one final turn. The draw pile never ends this game.

**Why play it:** a race with a clear finish line. Every wall counts, not only sets, so it plays faster than 3 Sets of 3 Walls.

**Watch out for:** anyone in the 40s. One more pair or set takes them over the line, and Debt can pull them back below it.

### 8.8 Timed Game

Choose **How long?** (10, 15, 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes) under the ending. The clock counts only time spent playing: if you close the page and carry on later, the time away doesn't count. The board shows the time left next to the actions, for example "3 actions left · 12 min left" (it is updated at the start of each turn).

When the time is up, at the start of the next turn the game says "Time's up! Everyone gets one last turn." That player and everyone else take one last turn, and then the most miles wins. The draw pile never ends this game.

**Why play it:** you know when the game will finish, so it fits a lunch break or a bedtime.

**In an online room** the host's time setting is used, and the room shows it, for example "Timed Game (20 minutes)".

### 8.9 The final round

When the end is reached, the game doesn't stop straight away. A big notice appears:

- **FINAL ROUND!** when a player completed 3 sets: "Final round! … Everyone else gets one final turn."
- **LAST CARD!** when the deck ran out: "The last card was drawn! Everyone else gets one final turn."

Press the notice's button to close it. Then every other player takes one more turn, in the usual order. The player who reached the end does not get another turn. When the turns come back around to them, the game is over.

**Use your final turn well:** build everything you can. Attacks still work, so a last Debt on the leader can change the result.

### 8.10 Who wins

The player with the most miles of wall wins. Section 11 explains how miles are counted.

If two or more players tie for the most miles, they share the win: the game says "It's a tie: *Name* & *Name*".

A game can also end early:

- **Everyone else left** an online game: "Everyone else left the game."
- **End Game** pressed on the game screen (section 9.6): the game stops without a winner.

### 8.11 The win screen

When the game is over, a red screen shows:

- **WALL COMPLETE** on a ribbon at the top, or **YOU WIN!** when someone playing on this device won. Then confetti falls and a short fanfare plays (no confetti when Card animations are off or your device asks for less motion; no fanfare when Sound is off).
- **The winner:** "*Name* wins!" or "It's a tie: …".
- **Why the game ended,** for example "Someone built 3 sets of 3 walls.", "The deck ran out and the final turns are done." or "Both the deck and 3 sets are done."
- **The final scores:** every player from most miles to fewest, each with a bar showing their miles.
- A wall of bricks building itself along the bottom.

## 10. Taking your turn

Every turn has three steps: draw 3 cards, take up to 3 actions, then press **End Turn**. An action is building a wall or playing a card.

### 10.1 The start of the game

When a game starts, the deck of 107 cards is shuffled and every player is dealt 5 cards, one at a time around the table. The rest of the deck becomes the draw pile. A random player goes first (in the Tutorial game, you always go first), and play then moves around the table in order.

If you are dealt a Play Immediately card (a card with a red title), you must use it at the start of your first turn.

### 10.2 Step 1: draw 3 cards

When your turn comes:

1. A gold band across the screen says "*Your name*'s turn" with an **OK** button. (In Pass'n'Play, the pass screen comes first and your turn starts as soon as you press **I'm *Name* - show me**.)
2. If anything happened to you since your last turn, the **WHILE YOU WERE AWAY** box lists it first. Press **OK, it's my turn**.
3. Press **OK**. Three cards fly from the draw pile into your hand.
4. If any card you drew is a Play Immediately card, it is shown big in the middle of the screen with its caption. Press **Continue**, and it takes effect straight away. It doesn't use an action.

![The turn banner](guide/50-turn-banner.png)

*The turn banner: press OK to draw your 3 cards.*

You now have 3 actions, shown as "3 actions left" at the top of the screen.

#### Skipped and limited turns

- **Quarantined:** if someone played Quarantine on you, you still draw your 3 cards, and then may block it with Self-Defense. If you don't, you skip the rest of the turn: a **QUARANTINE / TURN SKIPPED** notice says so. Press its button to let the game go on.
- **Democrat Border or Puppeteer:** if one of these was played on you, you get only 1 action this turn instead of 3. The Cards in Hand board shows **1 ACT** beside your name beforehand.

### 10.3 Step 2: take up to 3 actions

Tap any card in your hand. A popup shows the card big, with:

- **The card's name and what it does,** in plain words.
- **What you can do with it,** as buttons. The buttons depend on the card (table below).
- **A note** if you can't use it right now, explaining why.
- **Scan QR Code:** opens the card's web page with its video (section 15).
- **X** (top corner) or **Back:** closes the popup without doing anything.

| A **Wall Section**, and you hold a Man Hour | **Discard a Man Hour** | Builds this wall, paying with a Man Hour. 1 action. |
| A **Wall Section**, and you hold 2 other Wall Sections | **Pick 2 Walls to Discard** | Then tap the 2 other walls in your hand to pay with. A bar says "Pick 2 more walls to discard". 1 action. |
| A **Man Hour**, and you hold a Wall Section | **Use On Wall** | Then tap the Wall Section to build. A bar says "Pick the Wall Section to build with your Man Hour". 1 action. |

| **Savings** or **Self-Defense** | none | Note: "Savings is only used to pay off a Debt played on you." / "Self-Defense is only used to block an attack on you." |
| A card you can't use now | none | A note says why, for example "No actions left this turn.", "To build this you need a Man Hour or 2 other Wall Sections." or "You can use this card on your turn." |

#### Drag and drop

Instead of tapping, you can drag a card out of your hand:

- **Drag a Wall Section or Man Hour onto your walls** to build with it.
- **Drag an action card onto the discard pile** to play it.
- **Let go anywhere else** and the card goes back into your hand.

| --- | --- |

### 10.4 Step 3: end your turn

Press **End Turn** on the right. It turns green and glows when you have no actions left. The next player's turn begins.

When the draw pile runs out during anyone's turn, the discard pile is shuffled to make a new draw pile, and a message says so. (In the Exhaust Deck, Whatever Happens First and Whatever Happens Last endings, the deck running out can end the game: section 8.)

### 10.5 Your hand has no limit

There is no maximum hand size. Cards build up in your hand if you don't use them, but a big hand makes you a target: Redistribution makes the player with the most cards hand them all out, and Controlled Opposition and Stimulus Check steal cards from you.

### 10.6 A sample turn

Alex starts a turn with a Wall Section 5 already built.

1. Alex presses **OK** and draws 3 cards: Wall Section 5, Man Hour and Debt.
2. **Action 1:** Alex taps Wall Section 5 and presses **Discard a Man Hour**. Alex now has a pair of Wall Section 5s: 8 miles instead of 3.
3. **Action 2:** Alex taps Debt and presses **Play (1 action)**, then picks Sam's single Wall Section 12. Sam has no Savings, so the wall moves to Alex's walls: 3 more miles for Alex, 3 fewer for Sam.
4. **Action 3:** Alex has nothing else useful, so presses **End Turn**.

## 11. Building walls and scoring miles

Walls are how you score: each Wall Section you build is worth miles, and matching sections are worth far more together than apart. A complete set of 3 matching sections is worth 16 miles and can never be taken from you.

### 11.1 The Wall Section cards

There are 16 different Wall Sections, numbered 1 to 16, and 3 copies of each: 48 Wall Section cards in the 107-card deck. Each kind has its own picture of a different style of wall, so matching sections are easy to spot.

A Wall Section in your hand is worth nothing. It only scores once it is built in front of you.

### 11.2 How to build

Building a Wall Section uses 1 action and must be paid for in one of two ways:

- **Discard 1 Man Hour card,** or
- **Discard 2 other Wall Section cards** from your hand (any kinds).

The payment cards go to the discard pile; the wall you build goes in front of you. Section 10.3 shows the buttons step by step:

1. Tap the Wall Section, then **Discard a Man Hour** or **Pick 2 Walls to Discard**, or
2. Tap a Man Hour, then **Use On Wall**, then tap the Wall Section, or
3. Drag the Wall Section or Man Hour onto your walls.

There are 16 Man Hour cards in the deck, the most of any card, so you will usually have one.

### 11.3 How miles are counted

Each kind of wall you have built scores on its own, by how many of that kind you have:

| Matching sections of one kind | Miles | Name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3 | a single |
| 2 | 8 | a pair |
| 3 | 16 | a complete set |

Your score is the total over all your kinds.

#### Examples

| Walls built | Miles |
| --- | --- |
| One Wall Section 4 | 3 |
| Wall Section 4 and Wall Section 9 (two singles) | 3 + 3 = 6 |
| Two Wall Section 4s (a pair) | 8 |
| Three Wall Section 4s (a set) | 16 |
| Three Wall Section 4s and two Wall Section 9s | 16 + 8 = 24 |
| Three sets: 4s, 9s and 12s | 16 + 16 + 16 = 48 |

#### Why matching matters

Compare building the same two cards two ways:

- Two different walls: 3 + 3 = **6 miles**.
- Two matching walls: **8 miles**.

And three cards:

- Three different walls: 3 + 3 + 3 = **9 miles**.
- Three matching walls: **16 miles**, and they are safe.

Adding the second card of a kind is worth 5 more miles; adding the third is worth 8 more. The closer a kind is to a set, the more each new card is worth.

### 11.4 What can happen to your walls

Built walls don't always stay put. Singles and pairs can be:

| Card | What it does to walls | Can you block it? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Debt** | An opponent takes one of your built sections into their own walls. | Yes, with Savings |
| **Afghanistan Pull Out** | When you draw it, you must give one of your built sections to an opponent. | Yes, with Self-Defense |
| **Mostly Peaceful Riot** | When drawn, every player pulls one of their own built sections back into their hand. | Yes, with Self-Defense |
| **Chinese Spy Balloon** | Can remove a Trump Train protecting a pair. | Yes, with Self-Defense |

### 11.5 Safe walls

- **Complete sets of 3 are always safe.** Nothing in the game can take, give away or pull back a section that is part of a complete set.
- **Pairs protected by a Trump Train are safe** from Debt, Afghanistan Pull Out and Mostly Peaceful Riot (section 12, Trump Train). The pair shows a small Trump Train card on it. If the pair later becomes a set, the Trump Train is no longer needed and goes to the discard pile.

### 11.6 Sets and the game's end

In the 3 Sets of 3 Walls ending (and Whatever Happens First and Last), completing your third set reaches the end of the game (section 8). Each complete set shows a **×3** badge in a different colour on your walls, so you can count them at a glance.

### 12.2 Building cards

#### Wall Section 1 to 16 (48 cards)

The cards you score with. Build one for 1 action, paying with a Man Hour or 2 other Wall Sections (section 11.2). Matching sections score 3, 8 and 16 miles for 1, 2 and 3 of a kind.

**Tip:** don't build every wall you draw. Pay with mismatched walls to build the ones that match what you already have.

#### Man Hour (16 cards)

"The labor to build 1 Wall Section." Discard it to pay for building a wall. Tap it and press **Use On Wall**, then tap the wall to build.

**Tip:** Man Hours are the cheapest way to build. Abduction and Trafficking can take them from you, so use them rather than saving up lots.

### 12.3 Defence cards

#### Self Defense (5 cards)

Blocks an attack aimed at you. You never play it on your turn; instead, when someone attacks you and you hold one, the game asks whether to use it (section 13). It blocks every blockable card in the table above. It does not stop Debt (only Savings does), Stimulus Check, Fake News, Lawfare or Redistribution.

**Tip:** keep one if you can. It costs nothing to hold.

#### Savings (3 cards)

Pays off a Debt played on you, so you keep your wall. It can't be played by itself: the game asks whether to use it when someone plays Debt on you.

**Tip:** with a valuable pair on the board, a Savings card in hand is your insurance.

### 12.4 Play Immediately cards (red title)

These 8 cards happen as soon as you draw them (or at the start of your first turn if you were dealt one). They don't use an action and you can't keep them. The card is shown big in the middle of the screen with what it does; press **Continue** and it takes effect.

#### Abduction

You must discard a Man Hour from your hand. If you hold no Man Hour, nothing happens. You can block it with Self-Defense.

#### Afghanistan Pull Out

You must give one of your built walls to another player: you pick which wall (not one in a set of 3, and not a pair protected by a Trump Train), then pick which opponent gets it. It stays built on their board, so it scores for them. You can block it with Self-Defense. If you have no wall that can move, nothing happens.

#### Deep State

You hand out your whole hand around the table, one card per player in turn, starting with the player after you, and you choose which card each player gets. Cards that come back around to you return to your hand. You can block it with Self-Defense.

#### Fake News

Every player with more than the fewest miles gives one Wall Section, Trump or Trump Train card from their hand (their choice) to the player with the fewest miles. With several players tied for fewest, they receive in turn. If everyone has the same miles, nothing happens. It can't be blocked.

#### Lawfare

You hand out the Wall Sections in your hand around the table, one per player in turn. It can't be blocked.

#### Mostly Peaceful Riot

Every player, in turn, pulls one of their own built walls back into their hand (not from a set of 3 or a protected pair). Each player may block it for themselves with Self-Defense.

#### Redistribution

The player (or players) holding the most cards hands their whole hand out around the table. It can't be blocked.

#### Trafficking

You hand out your Savings, Trump, Trump Train and Man Hour cards around the table. You can block it with Self-Defense.

### 12.5 Action cards that build

#### Assassin

Build up to 2 Wall Sections from your hand for free: no actions and no Man Hours. Build them one at a time; you may stop after the first. You need a Wall Section in your hand to play it.

#### Legal Immigration

For 1 action, build up to 3 Wall Sections from your hand, with no Man Hours needed.

#### Voting Machine

For 1 action, build up to 2 Wall Sections from your hand, with no Man Hours needed. It works like a Man Hour that builds two walls.

#### Toilet Paper

Build matching walls straight from your hand: choose a kind you hold 2 or 3 copies of, and every copy of it in your hand is built at once. The first kind comes with the card's 1 action; each further kind costs 1 more action, and you may stop after any. You need at least 2 matching Wall Sections in your hand.

**Tip:** Toilet Paper with 3 matching walls in hand is an instant 16-mile set.

### 12.6 Action cards that attack

#### Debt (7 cards)

For 1 action, take one built wall from an opponent into your own walls. You choose the wall: any single or pair section, not one in a set of 3 and not a pair protected by a Trump Train. If the owner holds Savings, they may pay off the Debt and keep their wall (the Debt is still used up).

**Tip:** take a wall that matches one of yours: it scores more for you, and breaking up their pair costs them 5 miles.

#### Controlled Opposition

For 1 action, take 2 random cards from the hand of an opponent you choose. They can block it with Self-Defense.

#### Stimulus Check

Take 3 random cards from an opponent's hand. It uses no action and can't be blocked.

#### Chinese Spy Balloon

For 1 action, choose one of two effects:

- **Take:** pick an opponent and take all their Trump and Trump Train cards (up to 12).
- **Remove a Trump Train:** pick an opponent's protected pair and remove its Trump Train guard, so the pair can be attacked again.

The opponent can block it with Self-Defense.

#### Quarantine

For 1 action, pick an opponent: on their next turn they draw their cards but skip the rest of the turn. They can block it with Self-Defense when you play it, and again at the start of their turn. The Cards in Hand board shows **SKIP** beside them until then.

#### Democrat Border

Pick an opponent: on their next turn they get only 1 action. It uses no action. They can block it with Self-Defense. The board shows **1 ACT** beside them.

#### Puppeteer

The same as Democrat Border (the opponent gets only 1 action next turn), but it costs 1 action. They can block it with Self-Defense.

### 12.7 Action cards that draw or pick cards

#### Trump (4 cards)

For 1 action, discard it and draw 3 more cards from the draw pile.

#### Trump Train (3 cards)

For 1 action, choose one:

- **Draw:** discard it and draw 3 cards, like Trump.
- **Protect:** if you have a built pair (exactly 2 of a kind) that isn't protected yet, put the Trump Train on it. The pair is then safe from Debt, Afghanistan Pull Out and Mostly Peaceful Riot, like a set of 3, until it gets its third wall (then the Trump Train goes to the discard pile). A Chinese Spy Balloon can remove it.

#### Supreme Court Nominee

For 1 action, look through the draw pile and pick up to 3 cards into your hand. You have 30 seconds; press **Done** when you have picked. The draw pile is then shuffled.

#### Vote Recount

For 1 action, look through the discard pile and pick up to 3 cards into your hand. You have 30 seconds.

#### Freedom of the Press

For 1 action, pick up to 4 cards from the draw and discard piles together (30 seconds) and put them in jail, with the Freedom of the Press card. Jailed cards are out of the game for about 3 rounds; the board shows "Jail: *n* cards (*n* turns)". When released, they go to the discard pile. The draw pile is shuffled afterwards.

**Tip:** jail the cards your opponents need most: the Man Hours or the Wall Sections that would complete their sets.

## 13. Blocking with Self-Defense and Savings

When someone attacks you and you hold the right card, the game stops and asks whether you want to block. Self-Defense blocks most attacks; Savings pays off a Debt. Blocking uses up the blocking card but costs no action, and it works on anyone's turn.

### 13.1 Which card blocks what

| Attack | Blocked by | What blocking does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Debt | **Savings** | You discard the Savings and keep your wall. The Debt is used up. |
| Abduction | Self-Defense | You keep your Man Hour. |
| Afghanistan Pull Out | Self-Defense | You keep your wall. |
| Deep State | Self-Defense | You keep your hand. |
| Mostly Peaceful Riot | Self-Defense (each player for themselves) | Your walls stay built. |
| Trafficking | Self-Defense | You keep your Savings, Trump, Trump Train and Man Hour cards. |
| Chinese Spy Balloon | Self-Defense | You keep your Trump cards, or your Trump Train guard. |
| Controlled Opposition | Self-Defense | You keep your cards. |
| Democrat Border, Puppeteer | Self-Defense | You keep all 3 actions next turn. |
| Quarantine | Self-Defense (when played, or at the start of your turn) | You play your next turn as normal. |
| Stimulus Check, Fake News, Lawfare, Redistribution | Nothing | These can't be blocked. |

### 13.2 What you see

When a blockable attack is aimed at you and you hold the blocking card, a question box appears with the attacking card and a question such as "Block it with Self-Defense?" or "Use your Savings card to pay the debt?".

- **Answer yes** to block: your Self-Defense or Savings card goes to the discard pile, both cards fly across the board, and a message says the attack was blocked.
- **Answer no** to let the attack happen and keep your blocking card for later.

If you don't hold a blocking card, the game doesn't ask: the attack just happens.

### 13.3 Blocking in each game mode

- **Against bots:** the question appears on your screen during the bot's turn.
- **Pass'n'Play:** the pass screen asks for you first ("You have a decision to make."), you answer, and then the device goes back to the player whose turn it is.
- **Online:** the question appears on your own device. Everyone else sees "Waiting for *you* to decide whether to block it with Self-Defense…". If you don't answer in time, the game answers no for you.

### 13.4 Should I block?

- **Block attacks on your best walls.** A Debt on one of your pairs costs you 5 miles and gives your opponent at least 3. That's worth a Savings card.
- **Let small attacks go.** If Abduction would take a Man Hour you don't need, keep your Self-Defense for something bigger, like Quarantine.
- **Quarantine gives you two chances to block:** when it is played, and again at the start of your turn.
- **Bots block too.** Easy bots only block about half the time; Medium and Hard bots block whenever they can.

## 19. Strategy guide

The winning habit is simple: build matching sections, protect your pairs, and use Debt on the leader's pairs. The tips below go from the basics to the finer points.

### 19.1 The basics

- **Match, match, match.** A pair scores 8 (not 6) and a set scores 16 (not 9). Every new card added to a kind you already have is worth more than starting a new kind.
- **Finish pairs into sets.** A set of 3 can never be taken. Until then, your pair is a target.
- **Use all 3 actions when you can.** An unused action is a lost build or attack, unless you're deliberately saving a card.
- **Pay with walls you don't need.** When you have no Man Hour, pay with 2 Wall Sections that don't match anything you have built.
- **Keep one defence card.** A Self-Defense or Savings in hand costs nothing to hold and can save 5 to 8 miles.

### 19.2 Building smarter

- **Save Man Hours for matching walls.** Don't spend a Man Hour on a single you'll never pair; wait for a wall that matches.
- **Look at the discard pile.** If two copies of a kind are already discarded or built by others, that kind can't become your set.
- **Count the copies.** Each kind has only 3 copies. If an opponent has built one Wall Section 7, you can never finish a set of 7s without taking theirs.
- **Free builders are gold.** Assassin (2 builds, no action), Legal Immigration (3 builds, 1 action), Voting Machine (2 builds, 1 action) and Toilet Paper (whole sets, 1 action) let you build far more than 3 walls in a turn. Save them for when your hand has matching walls.
- **Toilet Paper plus 3 matching walls** is an instant, untouchable 16-mile set.

### 19.3 Attacking

- **Debt the leader's pairs.** Taking one wall from a pair costs them 5 miles and gives you 3 or more. Taking a single costs them only 3.
- **Debt walls that match yours.** If you have one Wall Section 9 and they have a pair of 9s, taking one gives you a pair (+5 for you) and leaves them a single (−5 for them).
- **Check for Savings first.** If they just drew lots of cards, they may hold Savings. Consider waiting until they have used it.
- **Quarantine the player who is about to win.** A skipped turn in the final rounds can decide the game.
- **Democrat Border costs you nothing.** It uses no action, so it is almost always worth playing on the leader.
- **Stimulus Check can't be blocked** and costs no action: take 3 cards from whoever has the biggest hand.
- **Chinese Spy Balloon** is strongest against a player hiding a pair behind a Trump Train, or a player holding several Trump cards.

### 19.4 Defending

- **Protect a valuable pair with Trump Train** when someone has been playing Debt. Hard bots do this; you should too.
- **Don't let your hand grow too big.** Redistribution, Controlled Opposition and Stimulus Check all punish big hands.
- **Spread your risk.** Two pairs are harder to wreck than one pair and several singles, because Debt takes one wall at a time.
- **Save Self-Defense for big attacks** (Quarantine, Afghanistan Pull Out on a pair) rather than small ones (Abduction of a spare Man Hour).

### 19.5 Reading the game

- **Watch the Miles of Wall board.** The leader is everyone's target; sometimes it pays to be second until the final round.
- **Watch the draw pile count** in Exhaust Deck, Whatever Happens First and Whatever Happens Last. When it gets low, build everything you can.
- **Count sets on every board.** The ×3 badges tell you who is close to 3 sets.
- **Use the game log** to remember who played what, and who might still hold Savings or Self-Defense.

### 19.6 The final round

- **Build everything.** There is no future turn to save cards for.
- **Attack the leader** with every Debt you hold: it still moves miles. Quarantine and Democrat Border are wasted on a player who has already had their last turn.
- **Count the score.** If you can't catch the leader, make sure you finish second.

### 19.7 Playing against bots

- **Easy bots never attack,** so you can build freely. They block only half the time.
- **Medium bots Debt you** as soon as they can. Protect pairs early.
- **Hard bots target the leader** and remember how you play. If you Debt them often, they protect their pairs with Trump Train sooner. Being quietly second can keep them off your back.

### 20.5 Rules questions

**Can I play a card on someone else's turn?** Only Self-Defense and Savings, when the game asks you whether to block.

**Do I have to use all 3 actions?** No. Press **End Turn** whenever you like.

**Is there a hand limit?** No, but a big hand is a target (section 10.5).

**Can I take back a card I played?** No. You can back out only while the game is still asking you to choose.

**Can Debt take a wall from a set of 3?** No. Sets of 3 are always safe, and so are pairs protected by a Trump Train.

**What happens when the draw pile runs out?** The discard pile is shuffled to make a new one, unless your ending says the deck running out ends the game (section 8).

**What if two players tie?** They share the win.

**Does Gold help me win?** No. Gold only adds extras like card backs and leaderboards; the rules are the same for everyone.

**Does the game work offline?** Games against bots and Pass'n'Play keep working if your connection drops after the page has loaded. Online play and signing in need the internet.
